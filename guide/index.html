How to use lastfmlists

This guide explains what everything on the site does. To get going quickly, read Getting started and Explore, then try a few of the recipes. The FAQ has short answers to common questions.

Getting started

Type your Last.fm username on the main page and click Load my data. The site downloads your whole listening history from Last.fm, one page at a time. While it loads, you see a preview of your top tracks or artists that fills in as pages arrive. When it’s done, you land on the Explore tab.

While small libraries load in seconds, libraries with hundreds of thousands of scrobbles can take a few minutes, because Last.fm only allows a few requests per second. The tab title shows the progress, so you can switch to another tab while you wait. On a computer, a “Notify me when it’s done” button shows up after 10 seconds, and it sends you a browser notification when your lists are ready.

Click Save Data to keep your history in your browser. Next time you load the same username, only the scrobbles you made since then get downloaded. You can also save in the middle of a load, and the next load continues where it stopped.

Explore

The Explore tab shows ready-made lists from your history, grouped by theme: your all-time and recent highlights, the year of your choice, streaks, records, milestones, when you listen (times of day, weekends, summer and the Christmas season), and lists to help you rediscover old favourites. Each box shows the top 5, and Show more shows the top 15.

Click a name to open its page. Click Open in Lists to open the full list on the Lists tab with all its settings filled in. From there you can change any filter, which is also a quick way to learn what the filters do.

Reading a list

On the Lists tab, the filters are in the panel on the left. Under List settings, “Generate lists of” picks tracks, albums, artists or scrobbles. Tracks, albums and artists are ranked lists. Scrobbles is your history itself, one row per play, sorted oldest or newest first.

“Sort lists by” decides how the list is ranked, see Sorts below. List length sets how many rows you see: the top 10, 25, 50 or 100, all of them, or a number you type.

Tick “Show unfiltered stats in parentheses” to see each row’s all-time rank and play count next to its filtered numbers. This is useful when you’ve filtered to one year and want to see how a song compares to the rest of your history.

Click any row to open its own page.

The address bar always holds the list’s settings. Copy it to share them: anyone who opens the link gets the same list for their own history once their data has loaded. To share the list itself, use Export Image.

Sorts

Scrobble count is the default: the things you played the most.

Number of days played, weeks played and months played count how many different days (weeks, months) you played something on. These favour the songs you kept coming back to over the ones you played 30 times in one evening.

Most plays in a row counts plays back to back, with nothing else in between. This is the “I had it on repeat” list.

Most days in a row, weeks in a row and months in a row are streaks: how many days (weeks, months) in a row you played something at least once. Weeks start on Monday.

Most scrobbles in a single day, week or month finds each item’s best day (week, month) on the calendar. Most scrobbles within X hours does the same with a sliding window, so a binge that started at 10 pm and went past midnight still counts as one. Within 24 hours and within 7 days (rolling) are shortcuts for the two most common windows.

First to X scrobbles ranks items by the date they reached X plays, earliest first. Fastest to X scrobbles ranks them by how long it took from the first play to the Xth.

Oldest and Newest average play date take the average date of all of an item’s plays. Oldest finds your long-time favourites, Newest finds what you only got into lately.

Time spent listening multiplies plays by track length. Percentage of global scrobbles shows how much of something’s total plays on Last.fm came from you. Both need Load Details first.

What X means

Some sorts need a number, and a box for it appears under the sort when you pick one. For First and Fastest to X it’s the play milestone, like 100 plays. For Within X hours it’s the length of the window. For the average play dates it’s the minimum number of plays an item needs to be included, so songs you played once don’t fill the list.

Filters

Filters decide which scrobbles count. Everything in the Artist, Album, Track and Time menus throws out the plays that don’t match, and the list is then built from what’s left. If you set Year to 2023, a list of your top tracks becomes your top tracks of 2023, counted only with plays from 2023.

A few filters ignore the others and always look at your whole history: the scrobble counts marked “your data”, the ranks in your top lists, tracks scrobbled per artist or album, year of first scrobble, and days since last scrobble. So Artist scrobbles at least 500 together with Year 2023 keeps your 2023 plays of artists you’ve played 500 times in total, not of artists you played 500 times in 2023. For that one, use Equations, where the counts follow the order of the steps.

Set up as many filters as you want across the menus, then click Apply Filters. A scrobble has to match all of them to count. The active filters show as chips above the list, and clicking a chip opens that filter so you can change it. Reset clears everything.

Artist, album and track

The three menus work the same way, each for its own name.

Artist name (album title, track title) matches one exact name. Capital letters don’t matter. Initial matches the first character, so B gives you everything starting with B.

Includes keeps names that contain a word or part of a word. Type it and press Enter or click Add. Words in the same group are alternatives: love and heart in one group keeps titles with either word. Add as new group adds a word that has to match as well, so love in the first group and remix in the second keeps only titles with both. Excludes works the other way round and leaves names out. Capital letters and hyphens are ignored.

Length and word count take a minimum and a maximum. Track title length from 1 to 4 finds the shortest titles you play. Track title word count from 8 up finds the longest ones.

Scrobbles (your data) and rank keep items by your all-time play count or their place in your top lists. Artist rank from 1 to 10 turns any list into “only my top 10 artists”, which works well with a time filter: your all-time top 10 artists’ songs from one year. If you want the top 10 artists of that year instead, Equations can do it: filter the year first, then the rank.

Tracks scrobbled per artist (per album) counts how many different tracks you’ve played by that artist (from that album). Set it to 1 on an artist list to find one-hit wonders, artists you only ever played one song by.

Year of first scrobble keeps the things you discovered in a given year. Days since last scrobble keeps items by how long ago you last played them: 365 and up finds the ones you forgot about.

The cap at the top of the Artist menu shows at most that many tracks or albums by the same artist, so one artist can’t fill the whole list. Set it to 1 on a track list for your top song by each artist, or on an album list for your top album by each artist. Tracks per album, at the top of the Album menu, does the same for albums on track and scrobble lists.

The last part of each menu needs Load Details. Global listeners and global playcount are how popular something is on all of Last.fm. Track global listeners under 10,000 is a quick way to find your most obscure favourites. Artist tags are genres, countries and other labels from Last.fm, and they use the same word groups as Includes: rock and metal in one group finds either. Tags are only as good as what people tagged on Last.fm, so smaller artists sometimes have none. Track duration is in seconds, so 600 and up finds the songs over 10 minutes.

Time

Played in the last keeps plays from the last 7, 30, 90 or 365 days, or any number of days you type.

Date range keeps plays between two dates, and both days are included.

Year, Month, Day of month and Weekday each take one or more values. Pick June, July and August in Month and you get your summer lists across every year. Add a Year and you get one specific summer. Saturday and Sunday in Weekday shows your weekend music.

Time of day keeps plays between two times, and it works past midnight: 22:00 to 04:00 gives you your late-night listening.

Position in your history uses scrobble numbers, where 1 is your very first scrobble. From 1 to 1000 shows what your first thousand scrobbles were.

Session and day starters

These look at what you start listening with.

Session starter keeps only the first scrobble after a long break. The break is 6 hours by default, and you can change it under Long gap before a session. With this on, a track list shows the songs you most often start a listening session with.

Day starter keeps the first scrobble of each day. First of the day (literal) takes the first play after midnight. First of the day (smart) also needs the long gap before it, so if you listened from 11 pm to 1 am, the song at 12:05 doesn’t count as the start of a new day.

Recipes

Some combinations to try. Anything not mentioned stays at its default.

Your top albums of one summer

Generate lists of Albums. In Time, set Year to the year you want and Month to June, July and August.

Songs you play late at night

Generate lists of Tracks. Set Time of day from 23:00 to 04:00. Tick Show unfiltered stats in parentheses: when the two play counts are close, that song is mostly a night song.

Artists you binged and dropped

Generate lists of Artists and sort by Most scrobbles in a single week. In the Artist menu, set Days since last scrobble to 365 or more.

Your longest streaks

Sort by Most days in a row for daily streaks, or Most plays in a row for songs on repeat. Add a Year to find the longest streak of that year.

Where your Last.fm started

Generate lists of Scrobbles, sorted Oldest first, to read your first plays in order. Or generate Tracks and set Position in your history from 1 to 1000 to see the top songs of your first thousand scrobbles.

One song per artist

Generate lists of Tracks, set Tracks per artist to 1 and List length to 50. You get your top 50 songs with no artist twice.

Your discoveries of a year

Generate lists of Artists and set Year of first scrobble to the year you want. The list shows the artists you found that year, ranked by how much you’ve played them since.

Hidden gems

This one needs Load Details. Generate lists of Tracks, set Track global listeners to at most 5,000 and Track scrobbles to at least 20. You get the songs you love that hardly anyone else listens to.

Load Details and Load ALL Details

Your listening history only has names and dates. Some filters and sorts need more than that: track length, tags, and how many listeners and plays something has on all of Last.fm. That’s what the two buttons at the top download.

Load Details gets this info for everything you play often: artists you’ve played more than 100 times, albums more than 10 times and tracks more than 5 times. It always covers at least your top 250 artists, 500 albums and 1,000 tracks. That’s enough for almost everything on the site.

Load ALL Details does the same for every artist, album and track you’ve ever scrobbled. It needs one request per item, and Last.fm only allows a few per second, so it can take hours on a big library. You don’t have to do it in one go: click Save Data whenever you want to stop, and the next time you click the button it only downloads what’s still missing.

Both buttons tell you how many items they will fetch and ask before they start. If everything already has details, they offer to download them again, since listener and play counts on Last.fm keep changing. When a run finishes, click Save Data so you don’t have to download the details again.

Charts and the bar chart race

Display mode switches between List, Bar Chart and Bar Chart Race. List length sets how many bars there are, and all your filters still apply.

Bar Chart draws the list as bars. Chart orientation puts the names down the side or along the bottom. The logarithmic scale helps when the top item is far ahead of everything else.

Bar Chart Race animates how the list changed over time. Pick a start and end date, how often it updates (every day, week or month) and the speed in milliseconds per step, where lower is faster. The race shows up to 20 bars, and the four artists who are on screen the most get their own colors, so you can follow their tracks and albums as they move. You can pick the colors yourself: click a swatch in the legend to change it, or "Color an artist" to add anyone by name, so your favourite group is always the same pink. Your colors stay in this browser and work in every race and bar chart, and a shared link brings them along. Untick "Color the top artists for me" if you only want your own picks colored. The slider next to the play button scrubs through the race. Weeks or months work best for long stretches of time, days for a year or less. Filters work here too, so you can race a single genre or just your weekend listening.

Graphs

The Graphs tab draws lines over time for any artists, albums or tracks you pick, up to eight at once. Click Add, pick artists, albums or tracks, and type a name, or click My top 5 to replace the lines with your top 5 of that kind. Click a color to change it; your colors are shared with the bar charts and races. The round buttons next to the lines start over with your top 5 artists and download the graph as a PNG.

Under the graph, Graph picks a ready-made graph: plays per month, total plays over time, all-time rank over the years, rank in each year, longest streak each month and days played each week. Period sets whether each point is a day, week, month or year. Every ready-made graph is just a set of the settings behind Customize, so you can start from one and change it.

Customize has the rest. Measure is any of the sorts from the Lists tab. Show picks between the value itself (how many plays, days or weeks) and the rank, which is the place it would have in that list. Count decides what each point looks at: In each period only counts that month (or week, day, year), Up to each period counts everything from the start until then. So Rank with Up to each period and Year is where a song stood in your all-time list at the end of every year, and Rank with In each period is its place in each year’s list on its own. Ranks shown sets how far down a rank graph goes (Auto goes just deep enough for your lines), Log spreads out the small values, and From and To limit the dates.

On the Lists tab, Graph over time opens the list on screen here: its top 5, ranked the way the list ranks them and counted only from the plays the list uses, so all its filters still apply. Use all plays drops the filters again. On an artist, album or track page, Graph its rank over time opens its all-time rank here, since the page already shows its plays. The download button saves the graph with its legend.

The address bar keeps the graph’s lines and settings, so you can share it as a link. Whoever opens it sees the same graph made from their own history, with your colors.

Comparison mode

Comparison puts two lists side by side. Turn it on with the Comparison button under List settings. While it’s on, Editing: Left list / Right list at the bottom of the filter panel chooses which side your changes go to. Each side has its own list type, sort, filters and equations. Display mode, list length and the chart settings are shared.

Some ideas: this year against last year, summer against winter, or your top tracks now against your top tracks from your first 10,000 scrobbles.

Equations

Equations are for things the filter menus can’t do, like comparing two fields with each other. They’re meant for people who like tinkering, and you never need them for the normal lists.

You build them from steps in the Equations menu. Click Add step and pick an operation:

  • Keep matching plays compares a field with a number, text or another field. With several of these steps, a play has to match all of them.
  • Order plays changes the order the later steps work in. On a track list it also sets the order of the list.
  • Keep first per value keeps a number of plays for each artist, album or other value. Play counts in later steps still count all the plays your filters kept.
  • Show value adds a field’s value to every row, so you can see what you sorted or filtered by.

Steps run from top to bottom, and the arrows move a step up or down. For maths, pick Calculation on either side of a condition. It takes two values and an operation (add, subtract, multiply, divide or remainder), and either value can be another calculation. Play counts, ranks and tracks per artist or album count the plays kept by the filter steps above them, so the order matters. Filter Play year = 2023 first and Artist play count at least 500 second, and you get the artists you played 500 times in 2023. Swap the two and you get your 2023 plays of artists you’ve played 500 times in total. While there are steps, they are the list’s only filters: the list is built from your whole history, and the Artist, Album, Track and Time menus are paused. The menus say so, and their chips above the list are crossed out. Your settings there stay and come back when you clear the steps. How to use steps, at the top of the menu, has a few examples you can load with one click. Click Apply Filters to run them.

Typing them instead

The Advanced text editor shows the same steps as text, one per line or separated by semicolons. filter followed by a condition keeps the matching plays (you can leave out the word filter), sort followed by a field and asc or desc orders them, unique followed by a field and a number keeps that many per value, and show followed by a field shows it. Fields are written with hyphens, like artist-name, track-name-length or year, and the list next to the box puts them in for you. Conditions use =, !=, <, <=, > and >=, maths uses +, -, *, / and %, and text goes in quotes. Months go from 1 to 12, and weekdays go from 0 for Sunday to 6 for Saturday. If the text has a mistake, the editor says where, and you can switch back to steps once it’s fixed.

One of the examples, as text:

artist-name-length = track-name-length
sort track-scrobble-count desc
unique track-name-length 1
sort track-name-length asc

Step by step: keep the plays where the artist name and the track title have the same number of characters. Sort them by how much you’ve played the track. Keep one track for each title length, which is your most played one since the list is already sorted. Then sort those by title length. You end up with your top song for every title length where the artist name is just as long.

A few more:

track-name = artist-name

Self-titled songs, where the track has the same name as the artist.

year - artist-first-scrobble-year >= 5

Plays from at least five years after you found the artist. On an artist list, this shows who you’re still listening to years later.

artist-name = "Radiohead"
sort track-name-length desc
show track-name-length

Radiohead songs from the longest title to the shortest, with the length shown on each row.

Artist, album and track pages

Click any artist, album or track in a list to open its page. It shows your scrobbles, its rank in your library, its rank among that artist’s tracks or albums, your first and last play, a link to its Last.fm page and up to three of its best placements. There’s also a graph of your plays over time.

Show all lists opens every ranking it appears in: each year, month and week, the last 7 to 365 days, the play milestones, the streaks and the other sorts. Click a ranking and that list opens with the item highlighted, at the same position the page said. The browser’s Back button takes you back to the page as you left it.

Exporting

Export Image saves the current list or chart as a PNG. You can pick a light or dark theme, and whether the active filters are printed on it. If the display mode is Bar Chart Race, Export Image saves the race as a GIF instead.

Export Grid makes an album cover collage from the current list. Set the number of columns and rows, up to 20 each. Allow duplicate covers lets the same cover show up more than once, which happens on track lists when several songs come from one album. Skip missing artwork leaves out items without a cover instead of leaving gaps. Show artist & album text prints the names on the covers.

For a classic 3x3 of your top albums: generate a list of Albums sorted by Scrobble count, add a time filter if you want one, then click Export Grid and set 3 columns and 3 rows.

Games

The Games tab opens once your data is loaded. The games only use the history you already have and don’t download anything.

Higher or Lower shows two artists, albums or tracks, and you need to pick the one you’ve scrobbled more. It gets harder over time.

Fill the List picks a top 10 list from your library, like “Top albums of 2021”, and you type as many of the ten as you can. You choose which kinds of lists come up, and there’s an optional time limit and a hard mode that hides the artist.

Put Them In Order gives you a few items to sort by how much you’ve played them. It starts with three and goes up to five as you keep winning.

Your best scores are kept in your browser.

Saving and privacy

Save Data stores your history, the lists built from it, album covers and any details you loaded in your browser’s storage. Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Every browser and device has its own copy, and clearing your browser’s site data deletes it.

When you load a saved username, the site only asks Last.fm for scrobbles newer than your saved ones, which is much faster. If you saved in the middle of a load, the next load downloads only the pages that were still missing.

The site counts anonymous visits with GoatCounter: which features get used, which outside sites people follow links to, and roughly how long visits last. It never sends usernames, filter values, artist, album or track names, or scrobbles. The code is on GitHub if you want to check.
